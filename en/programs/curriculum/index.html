Junior ESL Course

The level test on your first day of enrollment places you in a class, and as levels rise, textbook difficulty and the target grade level rise together.

Level structureLEVELS

Kids 1PhonicsAges 5–6

Can hear alphabet sounds and read easy words.

Kids 2PhonicsAges 6–8

Can read short sentences aloud and repeat them.

Level 1Pre-A1Elementary Grades 4–6

Can say their name, age and where they live in English.

Level 2A1Grades 5–7

Can speak and write a self-introduction of about five sentences.

Level 3A2Grades 6–8

Can write a paragraph about everyday topics like school and family.

Level 4A2+Grades 7–9

Can give their own opinion and add reasons.

Level 5B1Grades 8–10

Can talk at length about their own ideas on familiar topics.

Level 6B1+Grades 9–10

Can write a short essay and debate a topic.

Level 7B2High school · Advanced

Can read long texts and write an essay supported by evidence.

Level 8B2+High school · Advanced

Can debate social issues and write logically.

Level 9C1Highest level

Can understand English books and lectures and give presentations.

Pre-A1 to C1 are levels in the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR). Use them as a reference when comparing with other academies' levels.
