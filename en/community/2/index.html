2026 Summer Camp: A Parent's Review (J.Y.)

2026.08.20

"Is it really okay for a 10th grader to go to camp?" That was my first thought. Still, after talking it over carefully with my child, we decided to send them to a language camp in the Philippines. Their schedule didn't match the other kids', so they went alone, and they joined late, so I worried whether they would fit in. But after hearing my child talk about the camp on returning home, I felt glad we sent them.

Through the classes, my child gradually gained confidence in English and developed the attitude of speaking up even at the risk of making mistakes. Beyond English skills, I think they also learned consideration and cooperation by managing a set schedule on their own and living together with friends.

Since it was a short period, I feel the biggest achievement was not English ability itself but a reduced fear of English and the confidence to take on a new environment. I thank the head teacher and the Filipino teachers who carefully guided the children so they could stay safe and healthy during the camp. It was a meaningful time that gave my child learning, growth, and good memories.
